Gear Fundamentals

A gear is a circular mechanical part with teeth around its edge. When two gears mesh (touch) together, they transfer force and motion from one shaft to another.

2.2 📐

Gear Ratio Calculations

The gear ratio tells you how the speed and torque change between the input and output. This is the most important formula in this chapter!

$$i = \frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{z_2}{z_1} = \frac{d_2}{d_1}$$
igear ratio (transmission ratio)
n₁drive gear speed (RPM) — INPUT
n₂driven gear speed (RPM) — OUTPUT
z₁teeth on drive gear
z₂teeth on driven gear

🌟 The Golden Rule — Easy to Remember

Gear ratio  =  Driven gear teeth  ÷  Drive gear teeth
              =       z₂           ÷       z₁

Driven > Drive  →  i > 1  →  SLOW DOWN  (gain torque 💪)
Driven < Drive  →  i < 1  →  SPEED UP   (lose torque)
Driven = Drive  →  i = 1  →  Same speed (direction reverses)
✏️ Worked Example 1
Problem: Drive gear has $z_1 = 20$ teeth. Driven gear has $z_2 = 60$ teeth. Drive gear spins at $n_1 = 300$ RPM. Find the driven gear speed $n_2$.

Step-by-step solution:

Step 1 — Calculate the gear ratio:

$$i = \frac{z_2}{z_1} = \frac{60}{20} = 3$$

Step 2 — Find output speed:

$$n_2 = \frac{n_1}{i} = \frac{300}{3} = 100 \text{ RPM}$$
✅ Output speed = 100 RPM — slowed down by a factor of 3

How stages connect — the compound shaft
The driven gear of Stage 1 and the drive gear of Stage 2 are locked onto the same shaft. Because they are fixed together, they always spin at exactly the same speed. That shared speed is what "links" one stage to the next.

⚡ Motor shaft → Stage 1 drive gear meshes↓ Stage 1 driven gear same shaft! Stage 2 drive gear meshes↓ Stage 2 driven gear → 🎯 Output
$$i_{\text{total}} = i_1 \times i_2 \times i_3 \times \cdots$$
Multiply all individual stage ratios together to get the overall ratio
✏️ Worked Example 2
Problem: A 3-stage gear train. Motor speed = 900 RPM. Find the final output speed.

· Stage 1: drive $z_A=10$, driven $z_B=30$  →  $i_1 = 30 \div 10 = 3$
· Stage 2: drive $z_C=15$, driven $z_D=45$  →  $i_2 = 45 \div 15 = 3$
· Stage 3: drive $z_E=12$, driven $z_F=60$  →  $i_3 = 60 \div 12 = 5$

(Drive gear = the one being turned; Driven gear = the one receiving the turn)

Step 1 — Total gear ratio:

$$i_{\text{total}} = 3 \times 3 \times 5 = 45$$

Step 2 — Output speed:

$$n_{\text{output}} = \frac{900}{45} = 20 \text{ RPM}$$
✅ Final output = 20 RPM — reduced by a factor of 45! 🚀

Torque & Speed Relationship

Here's a key insight: power is conserved (ignoring friction). This means slowing down always increases torque — you trade speed for strength!

$$P = T \times \omega = \text{constant}$$
Ppower (Watts) — stays the same
Ttorque (N·m)
ωangular velocity (rad/s)

The Speed ↔ Torque Seesaw

You can't have more speed AND more torque at the same time!
🐇
Speed
↓ decreases
when driven gear has more teeth
⇌
💪
Torque
↑ increases
when driven gear has more teeth
$$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{n_1}{n_2} = i$$
✏️ Worked Example 3
Problem: Motor outputs 1000 RPM at 0.5 N·m torque. It passes through a gearbox with ratio $i = 10$. Find output speed and torque (assume 100% efficiency).
$$n_{\text{output}} = \frac{1000}{10} = 100 \text{ RPM}$$ $$T_{\text{output}} = 0.5 \times 10 = 5 \text{ N·m}$$
✅ Output: 100 RPM and 5 N·m — 10× more torque!

Speed Ratios & Angular Velocity

Converting RPM → Angular Velocity

$$\omega = \frac{2\pi n}{60} \quad \text{(rad/s)}$$
ωangular velocity (radians per second)
nrotational speed in RPM
2πone full revolution in radians
60seconds in a minute

Compound Speed Ratio — Exam Notation Exam: 2022Q3

$$I = \frac{n_1}{n_2} = \frac{\text{product of all driven gear teeth}}{\text{product of all drive gear teeth}}$$
For a 2-stage train with drive gears A, C and driven gears B, D:
$$I = \frac{z_B \times z_D}{z_A \times z_C}$$
n₁speed of the input (motor) shaft
n₂speed of the output shaft
z_A, z_Cteeth on each drive gear (one per stage)
z_B, z_Dteeth on each driven gear (one per stage)
This is just the multi-stage formula written as one fraction — useful in exams when all tooth counts are given in the diagram.

Quick Reference — All Gear Formulas

Pitch Circle Diameter
$$d = m \times z$$
Single-Stage Gear Ratio
$$i = \frac{z_2}{z_1}$$
Output Speed
$$n_2 = \frac{n_1}{i}$$
Output Torque (ideal)
$$T_2 = T_1 \times i$$
Multi-Stage Ratio
$$i_{\text{total}} = i_1 \times i_2 \times \cdots$$
RPM → Angular Velocity
$$\omega = \frac{2\pi n}{60}$$

Speed-Up vs Slow-Down at a Glance

Driven teeth  >  Drive teeth  →  Slow down, gain torque  ✅  (common in robots)
Driven teeth  <  Drive teeth  →  Speed up, lose torque
Driven teeth  =  Drive teeth  →  Same speed (direction reverses only)
🔑
Key insight: Most robot actuators use reduction gears (slow down) because motors spin fast but weakly. Gears trade speed for the extra torque needed to lift, push, or grip.

Practice Questions

Try each question before revealing the answer. Choose the option you think is correct, then click Show Working to check.

Question 1
A drive gear has 15 teeth and a driven gear has 45 teeth. The drive gear spins at 120 RPM. What is the output speed of the driven gear?
A 360 RPM
B 40 RPM
C 60 RPM
D 80 RPM
Step 1 — Gear ratio:
$i = z_\text{driven} \div z_\text{drive} = 45 \div 15 = 3$

Step 2 — Output speed:
$n_\text{out} = n_\text{in} \div i = 120 \div 3 = \mathbf{40 \text{ RPM}}$

The driven gear is bigger (more teeth), so it spins slower. ✅
Question 2
A gear has module $m = 2$ and 36 teeth. What is its pitch circle diameter?
A 18 mm
B 38 mm
C 72 mm
D 144 mm
Formula: $d = m \times z$

$d = 2 \times 36 = \mathbf{72 \text{ mm}}$

Remember: module controls tooth size, and two meshing gears must share the same module. ✅
Question 3
A robot motor outputs 0.4 N·m of torque and drives a gearbox with ratio $i = 8$. What is the output torque (assume 100% efficiency)?
A 0.05 N·m
B 0.8 N·m
C 2.4 N·m
D 3.2 N·m
Formula: $T_\text{out} = T_\text{in} \times i$

$T_\text{out} = 0.4 \times 8 = \mathbf{3.2 \text{ N·m}}$

Slowing down (ratio > 1) multiplies the torque by the same factor as the speed is divided. ✅
Question 4
A 2-stage gear train has the following gears:
Stage 1 — drive gear: 10 teeth, driven gear: 40 teeth
Stage 2 — drive gear: 12 teeth, driven gear: 36 teeth
The motor runs at 480 RPM. What is the final output speed?
A 120 RPM
B 60 RPM
C 40 RPM
D 20 RPM
Step 1 — Each stage ratio:
$i_1 = 40 \div 10 = 4$
$i_2 = 36 \div 12 = 3$

Step 2 — Total ratio:
$i_\text{total} = i_1 \times i_2 = 4 \times 3 = 12$

Step 3 — Output speed:
$n_\text{out} = 480 \div 12 = \mathbf{40 \text{ RPM}}$

The driven gear of Stage 1 and the drive gear of Stage 2 are on the same shaft — that's the "link" between stages. ✅
Question 5
A 3-stage gearbox has ratios $i_1 = 2$, $i_2 = 5$, $i_3 = 3$. The output shaft turns at 10 RPM. What is the motor (input) speed?
A 100 RPM
B 150 RPM
C 200 RPM
D 300 RPM
Step 1 — Total ratio:
$i_\text{total} = 2 \times 5 \times 3 = 30$

Step 2 — Work backwards:
$n_\text{in} = n_\text{out} \times i_\text{total} = 10 \times 30 = \mathbf{300 \text{ RPM}}$

This is the reverse of the usual question — if the output is known, multiply by the total ratio to find the input. ✅
