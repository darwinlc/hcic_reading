Q3
Amy's Missing Number
Number Theory
Amy added the numbers from 1 to n. She missed one number and the average of the remaining numbers is 35 5⁄17. What is the number missed?
Answer: 7
Step 1 — Convert the average & find n

Convert the mixed number: 35 5⁄17 = 600/17.

Since the average equals 600/17, the number of remaining terms must be a multiple of 17 (so the division is exact). So remaining count = 17k, meaning n = 17k + 1.

The average ≈ 35.3 should be near the midpoint of {1…n}, so (n+1)/2 ≈ 35, giving n ≈ 69. Try n = 69 (= 17×4 + 1 ✓, leaving 68 = 17×4 numbers).

Key Trick The denominator 17 is the clue — it tells you the remaining count must be a multiple of 17, which uniquely pins down n = 69.
Step 2 — Compute the total sum
S = n(n+1)/2 = 69 × 70 / 2 = 2415
Step 3 — Find the missing number

After removing the missing number m, 68 numbers remain:

Remaining sum = 68 × (600/17) = 4 × 600 = 2400 Missing number m = 2415 − 2400 = 7

Q4
Quadrilateral Area Problem
Geometry
In quadrilateral ABCD: EB=2AE, KC=2BK, DL=2LC, AG=2GD. Corner triangle areas: AEF=1, DGI=2, BHK=4, CLJ=3. Inner quadrilateral FHJI=30 cm². Find area of ABCD.
Answer: 60 cm²
Key Setup

Each division point cuts its side in ratio 1:2 from one vertex. Lines connecting these points create an inner quadrilateral FHJI, four corner triangles, and four "band" regions in between.

Core Insight Because each side is cut in ratio 1:2, every "band" region between a corner triangle and the inner quadrilateral has area exactly twice the adjacent corner triangle.

Why each band = 2 × corner

Take corner AEF (area = 1). Point E divides AB with AE:EB = 1:2. When you trace the triangle from E to the inner quadrilateral, the opposite base scales by factor 2 (from the 1:2 ratio on the adjacent side), so the band region has area = 2 × 1 = 2. The same logic applies to all four corners.

Band area = 2 × corner area ABCD = Σ corners + Σ bands + inner = (1+2+4+3) + (2+4+8+6) + 30 = 10 + 20 + 30 = 60 cm²
Q6
Even Pieces on Every Line
Combinatorics
On an 8×8 chessboard, place the maximum number of pieces such that every row, every column, and every diagonal contains an even number of pieces (including 0). Give an example.
Answer: 48
Why 48? — Degrees of Freedom Argument

Think of each square as a binary variable (0 = empty, 1 = piece). The constraints are:

  • 8 row-parity constraints
  • 8 column-parity constraints
  • Diagonal parity constraints

Freely fill the top-left 7×7 block (49 squares). Then the last column (7 cells) is forced by row parities, and the last row (7 cells) is forced by column parities. The corner (8,8) is automatically consistent. The diagonal constraints then remove further freedom, ultimately limiting the maximum to 48 filled squares.

Parity Logic Once 7×7 = 49 cells are chosen freely, the 8th row and 8th column are fully determined. The diagonal constraints mean we can't freely fill all 49 — some configurations must be left empty. The maximum satisfying everything simultaneously is 48.
Example Configuration (48 pieces)

Fill all 64 squares, then leave the main diagonal (top-left → bottom-right, 8 squares) empty — but adjust to also satisfy the anti-diagonal and all shorter diagonals.
