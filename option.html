2 · IV Trend — are buyers or sellers in control today?

In the header

What is IV (Implied Volatility)?

Think of IV as the price of insurance on an option. When lots of people rush to buy options (calls or puts), the price — and IV — goes up. When people are selling options or nobody is interested, IV falls.

Analogy: IV is like the cost of a home insurance policy. If a hurricane is coming (market uncertainty), insurance gets expensive (high IV). In calm weather, it's cheap (low IV).
IV ↑ (rising) — shown in amber

Buyers active

Someone is aggressively buying options — they expect a big move. This is a signal to look at the OI Jumps section next: which strike are they targeting?

→ Go to squeeze check if the bought strike is near-money.
IV ↓ (falling) — shown in blue

Sellers active

People are selling options and collecting premium. This often means market makers expect price to stay flat — a "covered call ceiling" is likely forming above spot.

→ The call wall is probably more reliable as resistance today.

IV 0.65 Normal range. No strong IV signal. Look at other signals for direction.

3 · P/C Ratio — overall market lean

P/C OI Ratio & P/C Vol Ratio

What is it?

P/C = (Total Put OI or Volume) ÷ (Total Call OI or Volume)

Puts are bets that the price will fall. Calls are bets that the price will rise.

  • P/C < 1 → more calls than puts → people are mostly bullish
  • P/C > 1 → more puts than calls → people are mostly hedging or bearish
Analogy: Imagine 1,000 people bet on a football game. 700 bet Team A wins (calls), 300 bet Team B wins (puts). P/C = 0.43 → strongly bullish crowd.

How to read the label

Ratio rangeLabel shownWhat it means
< 0.7BULLISH Heavy call buying. Options market is optimistic. Price often continues higher — but can also mean it's getting "too bullish" (contrarian warning).
0.7 – 1.0Neutral Balanced. No strong lean either way.
1.0 – 1.3Bearish lean More puts being bought/held. Some concern or hedging happening.
> 1.3BEARISH Heavy put buying. Options market is defensive. Can also signal a bottom if extreme (fear peak).
💡 OI ratio = accumulated positions (slower to change, shows longer-term positioning). Vol ratio = today's trading activity (faster, shows what's happening right now). If Vol ratio contradicts OI ratio, recent activity is shifting sentiment.

4 · Max Pain — where the market is "pulled"

Max Pain — shown in gold

What is Max Pain?

Max pain is the strike price where the most option contracts expire worthless — meaning option sellers (usually market makers and institutions) make the most money.

The theory: because dealers hold large hedging positions, there is subtle gravitational pull toward max pain as expiry approaches.

Analogy: Imagine 100 bets are placed at various prices for where BTC will be Friday. Most bets cluster around $103,000. The "house" (market makers) wins most if price ends up at $103,000. So they have an incentive to keep it there. That's max pain.

How to read the pull label

What you seePlain English
price 5.2% above → mild downward pull Current price is above max pain. As expiry nears, expect mild selling pressure back toward max pain.
price 3.1% below → mild upward pull Current price is below max pain. Expect mild buying pressure pushing up toward max pain.
near max pain → range-bound Price is already close to max pain. Expect choppy, sideways action — no strong directional pull.
⚠️ Max pain is most reliable within 7 days of expiry. For expiries more than 2 weeks away, treat it as a rough reference only — the pull is weak.

5 · OI Walls per Expiry — near-term barriers

Call Wall — shown in red Put Wall — shown in green

What is an OI Wall?

An OI (Open Interest) Wall is the strike price with the most outstanding option contracts for a specific expiry date.

  • Call Wall = the strike where the most calls sit. Acts as a ceiling — dealers sell stock as price approaches, pushing it back down.
  • Put Wall = the strike where the most puts sit. Acts as a floor — dealers buy stock as price falls, providing support.
Analogy: Think of the call wall as a glass ceiling and the put wall as a trampoline. Price tends to bounce between them, especially close to expiry.

Why is it shown per expiry now?

Previously, all expiries were added together. Problem: a June contract with 10,000 OI would overwhelm a May contract with 2,000 OI — making the May wall invisible even though it's what controls price this week.

Now each expiry has its own wall. Rule of thumb:

  • Nearest expiry = most relevant for current week's trading
  • Second expiry = useful context for medium-term direction

Reading the distance labels

LabelWhat it meansAction
near-money Wall is within 5% of current price. Strong, active barrier. High confidence the wall will hold. Use the SELL/BUY limit.
(no label) 5–15% from current price. Normal range. Wall exists but price has room to move before hitting it.
low priority Wall is more than 15% from current price. Mostly irrelevant for near-term trading. Note it as a long-term level.
★ significant OI More than 10,000 contracts at that strike. Unusually strong wall. This wall is less likely to break. Pay extra attention.
already breached ↑ Call wall is below current price — price blew past it. Bullish signal. Old resistance became support. Look for next call wall above.

6 · GEX Walls — smarter, gamma-weighted barriers

GEX Walls — shown in purple header

What is GEX?

GEX (Gamma Exposure) is a more sophisticated version of the OI wall. Instead of counting contracts equally, it weights each contract by its gamma — a measure of how much the contract forces dealers to hedge.

Analogy: Imagine two walls:

Wall A: 10,000 options expiring in 6 months, far from current price.
Wall B: 2,000 options expiring in 3 days, right at current price.

OI wall picks Wall A (bigger number). GEX wall picks Wall B — because those 2,000 near-expiry, near-money contracts force dealers to hedge much more aggressively right now.

GEX vs OI Wall — when to trust which

ScenarioTrust moreWhy
This week's price action (≤ 7 days) GEX Wall Near-expiry + near-money gamma dominates. GEX automatically focuses there.
Next month's direction OI Wall (2nd expiry) GEX has less signal for far-dated options. Raw OI is cleaner.
OI Wall and GEX Wall agree (same strike) Both High confidence barrier. Strong confluence = harder to break.
OI Wall and GEX Wall disagree GEX Wall GEX is picking up near-term hedging. OI may be dominated by far-dated noise.

7 · SELL / BUY Limits

→ SELL limit & → BUY limit — shown in green

What are these?

These are suggested limit order prices derived from the nearest expiry OI wall, with a small buffer (0.1–0.3%) so you don't place your order exactly at the wall (which everyone can see).

  • SELL limit = just below the call wall → sell before price hits resistance
  • BUY limit = just above the put wall → buy near support
⚠️ These limits only appear when the wall is on the correct side of spot (call wall above price, put wall below price). If the wall has already been breached, no limit is shown — the signal is no longer valid.
💡 Compare the SELL limit against the GEX Call Wall. If GEX agrees, the resistance is more credible. If GEX shows a different level, treat the SELL limit with more caution.

8 · OI Jumps — new money moving in

OI Jumps — shown below walls

What is an OI Jump?

OI (Open Interest) counts the number of active option contracts. When OI jumps significantly overnight, it means new positions were opened — someone put real money into a specific strike and expiry. This is one of the strongest signals because it shows where informed money is positioned.

Example: BTC260523C00105000 CALL $105,000 | +2,100 OI IV 0.35 → cheap, buy env

Someone opened 2,100 new call contracts at BTC $105,000 expiring May 23. IV is cheap, meaning they bought calls at a low premium. This is a bullish bet that BTC will hit $105,000 by May 23.

How to interpret OI Jumps

ScenarioMeaningAction
Large CALL OI jump, near-money, IV cheap Strong bullish bet at low cost Check squeeze probability. If ≤5% from spot and ≤5 DTE → potential squeeze.
Large PUT OI jump, near-money, IV cheap Downside protection being bought cheaply Bearish hedge being placed. Watch for price drop toward that put strike.
OI jump with IV rich (>1.0) Position opened at expensive premium Less efficient bet. Could be a hedge rather than a directional trade.
OI jump far from spot (>15%) Far out-of-money lottery ticket Low probability. Monitor but don't trade against it.

Threshold: only jumps ≥ 500 contracts are shown to filter out insignificant noise.

9 · Unusual Volume — activity spikes

Unusual Volume — shown at bottom

What is Unusual Volume?

This flags options where today's trading volume is more than 2× the open interest. Normally vol/OI ≈ 0.1–0.5. When it exceeds 2×, it means far more contracts traded today than the total outstanding — a clear sign of unusual activity.

Example: vol 4,200 / OI 1,800 (2.3×)

4,200 contracts traded today, but only 1,800 are held total. Someone is actively opening and/or closing large positions right now.

Filter: only options with OI ≥ 10 are considered — this removes single-contract trades (OI = 1) that would create fake 100× ratios.

Unusual Volume vs OI Jump — what's the difference?

SignalWhat it capturesTimeframe
OI Jump New positions being held overnight — conviction bets Persistent (shows up for days until positions close)
Unusual Volume High activity right now — could be opening OR closing positions Intraday snapshot (resets at market open)
💡 When the same option appears in both OI Jumps AND Unusual Volume, it means someone opened a big position and is actively trading it today. That's the strongest signal.

10 · Squeeze Probability

⚡ Squeeze — shown inside unusual volume rows

What is a short squeeze (options-style)?

When a heavily-bet call strike is very close to the current price AND expiry is just days away, option sellers (who are short the calls) must buy the underlying to hedge as price approaches the strike. This buying pressure can accelerate the move — a squeeze.

Conditions for HIGH squeeze probability:

✅ Strike is within 5% of current price (near-money)
✅ Expiry is within 5 days (near-expiry = high gamma)

If only one condition is met → LOW probability (shown in grey).
LabelMeaningWhat to do
⚡ HIGH squeeze probability Both conditions met. Price is near a strike that expires very soon with large OI. Strong signal. If it's a call, consider riding or buying. Tight stop.
near-money (DTE > 5) Strike is close but expiry is more than 5 days away. Time to squeeze is limited. Monitor. Squeeze could still happen but less urgency.
expiry near (gap > 5%) Expiry is very soon but the strike is still far from price. Low probability. The contract will likely expire worthless at current levels.

11 · Decision Flowchart — putting it all together

Use this flow each morning when you open the email for a ticker.

1
Is there an OI Jump ≥ 500 near-money (within 15% of spot)?
Yes → investigate (continue to step 2) No → skip to P/C ratio for background lean
Large OI jumps = someone placed a real bet. Worth understanding before trading.
2
What is the IV Trend in the header?
IV ↑ (rising) → buyers confirmed, go to step 3 IV ↓ (falling) → sellers active → covered call ceiling likely forming IV → flat → check price action (is stock rising or falling?)
3
What is the IV level for that specific option?
IV > 1.0 (rich) → seller environment; buyer paying expensive premium IV < 0.5 (cheap) → buyer getting a good deal; trust the direction more
4
If it's a CALL with IV ↑ and cheap premium — run the squeeze check:
Gap to strike ≤ 5%? Days to expiry ≤ 5?
Both yes → ⚡ HIGH squeeze probability One or neither → LOW probability, wait for confirmation
5
Does the GEX Wall agree with the OI Wall?
Same strike → high confidence barrier, use the SELL/BUY limit Different strike → trust GEX for this week, adjust limit accordingly
6
Post-expiry check (next day after expiry):
Next cycle OI rising AND IV rising → thesis intact, roll position OI flat/falling → smart money exited, step aside
